## Edition

- *Doctoris ecstatici D. Dionysii Cartusiani Opera omnia in unum corpus digesta ad fidem editionum Coloniensium, cura et labore monachorum sacri Ordinis Cartusiensis*, tomus XLI, *Opera minora* IX, Tournai, Typis Cartusiae S. M. de Pratis, 1912. The volume's contents page (printed page 8) lists: De Oratione p. 9, De Meditatione p. 69, De Fonte lucis ac semitis vitae p. 91, De Contemplatione libri tres p. 133, Sonus epulantis p. 291, Inflammatorium divini amoris dialogus p. 315, De Perfectione caritatis dialogus p. 345, De Particulari judicio, and the rest of the minor works ([source](../../raw/theology/saints/2026-09-09-denis-the-carthusian-de-meditatione-opera-omnia-41.md), frontmatter and the [p. 69] marker). *De oratione* (pp. 9–68) is not filed.
- Public domain in the United States: published 1912, before 1929; HathiTrust rights code `pdus`. Google, which digitised the Cornell copy, asks that its images and OCR not be re-hosted, redistributed or used commercially, and HathiTrust's access and use policy states that there are no restrictions on text transcribed, paraphrased or translated from them. The OCR therefore stays in the vault, which is not published (`/vault/*` is a forced 404 on the site); what the app ships is the English rendering.
- Source: <https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=coo.31924066451828>, read page by page through the text-only view (`cgi/ssd?id=coo.31924066451828;seq=N`, seq = printed page + 4), 222 pages fetched 2026-09-09 at a two-second pace, four of them on a second pass after a 403. The same volume is on Google Books in full view (ids `WBe_5ib5_xwC` and `iC4TbW9BOpUC`, both dated 1912) and is not on the Internet Archive, whose run of the set stops at volume 38.
- *De contemplatione* was first printed at Cologne in 1534 and, by the accounts found, not again until the Order's edition; no digitised copy of the 1534 printing was found on Google Books, the Munich digital collections, Gallica, e-rara or PRDL.

## What the raw files hold

Each file carries the printed page number in brackets before every page, and the page's OCR as it came, including the running scripture references that the edition sets in the margin and the OCR folds into the line ends ("Prov. iv, 23.", "Matth. v, 8."). The edition is set in two columns; where an article heading falls mid-column the OCR sometimes interleaves the other column's text into the title line.

**De meditatione** (pp. 69–90): half-title, blank, then the proemium (p. 71, "Cogitationes meae variae succedunt sibi", Job xx) and fourteen articles: I De nocumento cogitationum pravarum (p. 73); II De his quae salubriter ac jugiter sunt meditanda (73); III Increpatio ignaviae, negligentiae et irreverentiae (74); IV Quod in meditationibus ordo servandus sit (75); V De profectu hominis in salutari scientia (77); VI De compendiosissimo modo obtinendi sapientiam salutarem (78); VII De incessabili mentis defixione in Deo (80); VIII Prosecutio hujus materiae (81); IX De sui ipsius examinatione (84); X De meditandis circa Filii Dei incarnationem (85); XI De meditanda Christi conversatione (85); XII De Christi passione (87); XIII De excellentia gratiae a Christo distributae (88); XIV Deploratio malorum Ecclesiae (89). The colophon: "istud opusculum anno vitae meae sexagesimo septimo finio, anno Domini millesimo quadringentesimo sexagesimo nono … FINIS OPUSCULI DE MEDITATIONE" (p. 90).

**De fonte lucis ac semitis vitae** (pp. 91–132): proemium (p. 93, Osee xiv, "Quis sapiens, et intelliget ista?") and twenty-five articles on the three ways: I the many ways of Scripture (95); II Christ as the purgative, illuminative and perfective way (96); III–VII the purgative way, its daily exercise, meditation on the Passion, and the priest before, in and after celebration (98–103); VIII–XI the illuminative way and what contemplation is and its kinds (105–108); XII–XIX the unitive way, the twofold contemplation of the Deity and mystical theology, deifying love, the Bridegroom's effects in the contemplative soul, the marks of ecstatic love (110–121); XX–XXV what the doctors have written reduced to the three ways, servants, hirelings and secret friends, the three degrees of inner reformation, truth and life in their distinctions, and the industries by which the grace of contemplation is reached (123–129). Ends "FINIS OPUSCULI DE FONTE LUCIS AC SEMITIS VITAE" (p. 132).

**De contemplatione libri tres** (pp. 133–290):
- Liber primus, *De contemplatione et speciebus ac practica ejus* (p. 135), 71 articles: the intention of the work, the kinds of wisdom, what contemplation is (I–III, 136–137); the rational soul, synderesis, the intellect as its subject, the philosophers on contemplation (IV–X, 138–143); the contemplative and active lives compared (XI–XVIII, 144–153); the threefold way to contemplation and the daily exercise of those who aspire to it (XIX–XXV, 154–164); then the four kinds of contemplation: of the Trinity (XXVII–XXXVII, 166–178), the natural contemplation of the simple Deity (XXXVIII–XLIV, 179–186), of Christ, his incarnation, life, Passion, the Sacrament and his beauty (XLV–LV, 188–203), and of the goods of grace, of glory, of the heavenly country and of the created universe (LVI–LXXI, 205–228).
- Liber secundus, *De contemplatione secundum sanctorum Patrum atque doctorum praecipuorum sententias* (p. 231), 11 articles: Dionysius and the angelic motions, Origen, Augustine and Bernard, Hugh and Richard of St Victor, the "Doctor sanctus" (244) and the "Doctor devotus" (245) [NEEDED: confirm from the articles which doctors Denis means by these epithets; Thomas and Bonaventure are the usual readings], Ruysbroeck (247), Thomas Gallus of Vercelli (251), and Guigo de Ponte (252).
- Liber tertius, *De contemplatione Dei per sapientiam unitivam theologiamve mysticam* (p. 255), 25 articles: unitive wisdom and mystical theology defined and distinguished from speculative theology (I–III); the fourfold ascent and contemplation by negation (IV–IX); whether mystical theology is the gift of wisdom, and whether love or knowledge (X–XV); deifying love, rapture and ecstasy, the saints' raptures including Francis (XVI–XX); prayer and devotion belonging to unitive wisdom, God's hidden sons, whether contemplation in this life can reach the vision of God, and the divine, deiform, deified life (XXI–XXV). Ends "FINIS TRIUM LIBRORUM DE CONTEMPLATIONE" (p. 290).

## The translation question, as checked 2026-09-09

- denisthecarthusian.org lists *On Meditation*, *On Contemplation* (books one to three) and *On the Font of Light and Path of Life* as "Future Translation"; its completed translations are a devotional on the purgative way and the *Declaration to His Superior*. Its source-texts link is the UPenn Online Books listing of the Opera omnia.
- Documenta Catholica Omnia has no Denis the Carthusian (only the Areopagite, the Alexandrian, Exiguus and the pope of that name). PRDL lists 109 titles in 209 volumes, none of them these three or the 1534 *Opuscula*. No transcription corpus (Corpus Corporum, the Library of Latin Texts, Cartusiana) was found to carry the Latin as text.
- Ní Riain's 2005 translation (Four Courts) is in copyright.

## Caveats

- The OCR is Google's, of a clean 1912 typeface: letters are almost always right, but the marginal references land inside the lines, the two columns are read left then right so a heading can be split, and long words are hyphenated at the printed line ends. Read the page scan at the source URL before quoting.
- The 1534 Cologne printing, the only earlier edition, is not filed; the Order's text is "ad fidem editionum Coloniensium".
